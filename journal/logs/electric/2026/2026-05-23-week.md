## Session Prose

Three sessions, 2 days, ~75 minutes total. No practice May 17–20 or May 23 — this was effectively a late-week launch, not a full training week. That's context, not an excuse: the curriculum started May 21st and all three sessions ran.

**Session 1 — 05-21, 13:00 (~25 min):** First session ever. Covered tuning & posture, open string naming, A chord intro, and first theory block (7 prime notes + open string names). A chord took 12 minutes to get to 3 consecutive clean sets of 4 downstrums — initial buzz traced to index not sitting close enough to fret wire, occasional muting from ring finger lying too flat. Both fixed within the session. Open string names (E A D G B E) required no repetition — locked immediately. Overall: 2/4.

**Session 2 — 05-21, 18:32 (~25 min):** Same day, second session. Introduced the chromatic 1-2-3-4 warm-up — frets 1–4 unreachable without hand shift at this stage, so moved to frets 5–8, 2 clean passes. A chord anchor drill: round 1 scored 7–8/10, round 2 hit 10/10. Index-first placement sequence now deliberate and consistent. Theory: sharps, flats, "BE" mnemonic — all three check questions answered correctly, locked in. Status advanced to `advance` — ready for D chord. Overall: 2/4.

**Session 3 — 05-22, 10:17 (~25 min):** D chord introduced. Placement drill: 10/10 on the very first attempt — the shape landed without wrestling. A→D change then tested with both fingering variants: Variant B (middle-finger anchor first, which carries naturally to D chord's G-string root) was clearly cleaner. Hit 3 consecutive clean changes with Variant B. Issues: D chord placement breaks down when not watching the fretboard (expected at session 3), and strum attack occasionally catches string 5 on D or string 6 on A. Theory: enharmonics — both check questions correct, locked in. Overall: 3/4.

## Coach's Notes

**What's actually improving vs. what only feels like progress:**

The A chord shape landing is real. 10/10 on the anchor drill in session 2 is a concrete data point, not a feeling. The D chord 10/10 on first-ever placement drill is also real — and slightly remarkable for session 3. Theory retention is genuinely strong: five topics in three sessions, zero re-teaching needed.

What's less clear is the A→D *transition*. Three consecutive clean changes sounds like progress, but "clean at slow deliberate tempo while watching the fretboard" is still early-stage. The muscle memory for this change hasn't been tested under any time pressure yet and will likely feel uncertain when the metronome is introduced. Don't mistake "shapes are correct" for "change is functional."

**Curriculum pace:** Appropriate for week one. The JustinGuitar ladder says E chord is next after A→D is stable. Don't rush to E — A→D needs more reps first.

**Theory pace:** Theory is running ahead of the guitar technique in terms of ease. That's fine — it builds context without slowing technique work. The next theory step (chromatic scale on the guitar string by string) will start bridging the two.

**Session frequency:** 2 active days out of 7, but this was the inaugural week. The 25-minute daily target wasn't hit — average was closer to 37 minutes on active days (two sessions in one day counts as two active sessions but still only one active day). The real test is week 2: can sessions happen on at least 4–5 distinct days?

**Strum string accuracy** is a low-key issue that will compound if not drilled early. The pause-before-strum habit needs to become automatic before speed enters the picture.

## Top 3 Priorities for Next Week

1. **A→D change at the metronome — 50 BPM, 20 consecutive clean changes.** One change per beat, Variant B fingering, pause-before-strum on each chord. Track total reps per session. Goal: 20 clean consecutive changes at 50 BPM by end of week, then bump to 60.

2. **Daily sessions — minimum 4 distinct days.** Target remains 25 min/day. Two sessions in one day doesn't substitute for showing up on 4 separate days. Consistency over volume.

3. **Chromatic scale drill (theory + fretboard).** Verbally: name ascending C–C# –D–D#–E–F–F#–G–G#–A–A#–B–C without gaps or hesitation. On guitar: play it ascending and descending on string 6. This bridges the theory that's already locked to a physical fretboard map.
