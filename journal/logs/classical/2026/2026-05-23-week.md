## Coach's Notes

Three sessions is a fine start for week 1. The quality is high — every session was full mode with strong readiness numbers (the second session on May 20 was E4 F4, which is as good as it gets). The issue: both practice days were on the same two-day window (May 20–21), and there was no practice on May 22 or May 23. That's a 2-day gap before this review. For a beginner building motor patterns, daily or near-daily reps matter more than session length — muscle memory consolidates best with frequent short exposures, not two heavy days in a row followed by a gap.

The Moderato result is genuinely promising. A clean first-pass hands-together run at 40 BPM on a new piece, in session 3 of the entire journey, is solid. But "clean at 40 BPM on first contact" is not the same as "settled." Werner's rule — stay with pieces after they're playable so they settle — applies here. Resist the urge to call Moderato done and move on after one good run. Two clean passes at 50 BPM with the metronome is the actual bar.

The i-m alternation resolution was fast and looks real. The test will be whether it holds as pieces introduce more simultaneous demands (left-hand position shifts, new notes, reading harder rhythms). Keep monitoring, but no flag this week.

One recurring miss: the right-hand position baseline video was flagged in the coach note of sessions 2 and 3 and still hasn't been recorded. This is worth doing early — it's harder to correct a habitual forearm placement problem months in than to catch it now on video. This is a priority, not optional.

No recordings exist yet in the recordings folder. No comparative analysis is possible this week. From week 2 onward, record at least one take of Moderato per session and save it — even a phone audio file. This data will matter for the weekly review.
