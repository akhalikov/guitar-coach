## Why this song is here

- Nuno Bettencourt's arrangement is *the* reference piece for percussive acoustic fingerstyle — the muted slap between notes is a whole technique in itself
- All open chords, no barre — the difficulty is entirely in the right hand (rhythm, muting, dynamics), not the fretting hand
- A natural next step **after** Dust in the Wind: that song builds the steady thumb; this one adds percussion and syncopation on top

## What you're building

**Technique**
- **Percussive muting** — the "chuck": the picking hand slaps the strings to make a muted drum hit between chords. The signature sound of the song
- **Syncopation** — accents land off the beat; counting out loud ("1 & 2 & …") is essential
- **Dynamics** — the song lives on soft/loud contrast; it falls flat played at one volume

**Theory**
- G major diatonic chords (G, Am7, C, D, Em) — a clean worked example of chords-in-a-key, which lines up with JustinGuitar Grade 2 (Chords in a Key) and Stine's major-scale/diatonic material
- Slash chords G/B and D/F# — bass-note movement (voice leading) under familiar shapes

## 🎯 Focus when working on it

- **Right hand first, silent.** Practice the strum-and-chuck pattern on muted strings before adding chords — get the percussion consistent on its own
- **Count out loud.** The rhythm is the whole song; if you can't say the count, you can't play it clean
- **Very slow.** Half tempo or less. Speed is the last thing to add, long after the pattern and muting are reliable
- **One phrase at a time.** The intro/verse riff is the anchor — loop it in isolation before attempting the full song
